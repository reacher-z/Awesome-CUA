Open problems and a research agenda

The preceding comparisons suggest a connected research agenda. Better grounding, stronger planning, larger training corpora, and faster inference can each improve part of a system, while changing the assumptions under which the whole system is evaluated. The following directions are proposals derived from the synthesis. They should be tested through controlled studies rather than treated as established consequences of the catalog statistics.

Generalization needs several axes

A useful next step is temporal transfer: freeze training and artifact versions, then test on documented interface changes. Combine this with the task-, site-, app- and device-level distinctions in the evaluation section. State whether failures arose from changed controls, unavailable functionality or mistaken intent. This would make the intended meaning of “generalist” explicit rather than treating a single aggregate score as evidence for every form of transfer.

Measure the system that actually runs

An action policy is embedded in a runtime that parses observations, handles waits and timeouts, invokes tools, maintains state, and decides when to stop. The efficiency survey's accounting across observation, memory, action, and runtime motivates measuring this complete path. Evaluation should distinguish time spent producing a decision from time spent reaching a correct application state. It should also record where failures arise: a model error, an invalid action contract, an unavailable application, or an evaluator that cannot recognize a valid outcome. These categories support diagnosis without automatically excusing any failed deployment run.

There is also a need for calibrated uncertainty at several levels. A well-formed action can target the wrong object; a high-confidence decision can rely on an incomplete candidate set; a fluent completion report can contradict the environment. A research program should therefore examine the relationship between uncertainty in perception, action selection, outcome verification, and escalation. The relevant target is the quality of the resulting decisions under a declared cost and authority model, rather than an isolated confidence value. The trustworthy-GUI review's stage-based analysis provides a useful starting point for that decomposition.

Make evidence maintenance part of research infrastructure

A rapidly changing field needs correction mechanisms as well as new entries. Useful infrastructure would retain the relationship between a paper revision, a dataset release, an evaluator revision, and a result observation. It would expose unresolved differences instead of silently selecting the most convenient value. Stable record identifiers and versioned exports make this tractable for people and retrieval agents, while documentation of intended uses and measurement conditions keeps those exports interpretable. This extends the practical motivation of data and model documentation to the interconnected artifacts of computer-use research.

Scope and sources

The review's selection draws on a public, predominantly English-language catalog, its discovery sources, and the terms used in supplementary searches. Metadata-only records are preserved as discovery evidence. The catalog's year and topic distributions reflect its selection and annotation process.

Primary reading targeted relevant sections of representative works. Some sources are preprints and may change. Each reported result keeps the provenance of the paper or repository that reports it. Comparisons emphasize experimental contracts and mechanisms.

AI assistance was used in selection, extraction, and synthesis, with version checks, explicit source receipts, claim-level notes, and separate review states. The machine-readable ledgers are provided to enable corrections and further verification.

Conclusion

Computer-use agents unite interface perception, action selection, learning, and runtime control. Progress is easiest to interpret when the available observations, permitted actions, learning signal, evaluation protocol, and artifact version are explicit. A grounding score, a demonstrated next-action prediction, a successful interactive task, and acceptable assistance to a person answer different questions. This survey connects those questions through representative primary reading, comparisons that retain experimental conditions, and a reproducible inventory of research assets.

The practical direction is to improve both capability and the evidence supporting its use. Research collections can help by connecting papers to precise artifacts, preserving review states and unknowns, and offering consistent interfaces for readers, retrieval systems, and agents. The resulting knowledge base is valuable when it makes a claim easier to inspect and a resource easier to reuse. Its breadth remains a continuing, auditable objective.
