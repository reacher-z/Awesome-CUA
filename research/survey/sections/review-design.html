Review design and evidence base

This article is a structured narrative survey with a reproducible search trace and a deliberately selected primary-reading set. Its organizing questions are: RQ1, how do observation and action interfaces constrain agent design; RQ2, what do demonstrations, synthetic data, and reinforcement learning teach; RQ3, which capabilities do current evaluations actually measure; and RQ4, how can research assets remain useful to both people and software agents? The aim is to connect methods, measurement, and reuse.

A frozen inventory and a supplementary search

The starting inventory is the AwesomeCUA v2026.10.18 data release. It contains 3,048 active resource identities, of which 2,599 are papers or surveys. Of these bibliographic records, 1,123 have catalog editorial review and 1,476 retain a metadata-only state. The remaining 449 active resources describe implementations, models, datasets, benchmarks, tools, infrastructure, products, or publishers. A paper and its implementation are different resources, so the 1,572 reviewed-resource total counts both. Editorial review establishes the scope and source of a catalog annotation.

The supplementary query broadened the catalog's existing terminology with mobile-agent, UI-agent, desktop-agent, and screen-grounding phrases. It was executed on 18 October 2026, sorted by first submission, with 100 entries per page. The service reported 1,452 matches, and the retrieved union contained exactly 1,452 unique identifiers. This establishes pagination coverage for that query. Phrase matching also returned unrelated work on quantum computation and physical or network-mobile agents. The exact expression, timestamps, response hashes, returned identifiers, and deduplication outcome are supplied in the search ledger.

All 303 titles absent from the active inventory were inspected for this supplementary selection. Twenty-six ambiguous or potentially relevant abstracts received a complete abstract read; 15 concerned interface agents, their evaluation, or an adjacent interface-testing application. Eleven were not selected because the abstract described a different domain or did not establish material software-interface interaction. These are manuscript selection decisions. The other 277 results were not advanced beyond title screening. Additional targeted searches and reference following supplied historical, platform-specific, and method-specific primary sources. Their query and reading records are retained separately.

Selection and extraction

Representative primary reading was selected for coverage of interface families, observation modalities, action abstractions, training procedures, evaluation settings, and documented failure modes. Earlier work was retained when it introduced a still-relevant representation or evaluation contract. Newer work was used to examine developments such as runtime optimization, online learning, proactive assistance, and typed action selection. Selection did not use citation counts, repository stars, or reported leaderboard position as a quality threshold. The prior-survey comparison inspected all 15 survey abstracts and selected eight for relevant full-text sections. Bibliographic identity, research contribution, and artifact availability were extracted as separate fields.

For each source used in a substantive comparison, the reading ledger identifies the primary URL, retrieved or retained version, response fingerprint, sections or pages actually read, supported claims, and qualifications. An abstract-only record is labeled accordingly. The original source remains the authority for its reported results; this article contributes a synthesis and a descriptive inventory analysis. AI assistance was used for retrieval, extraction, drafting, and cross-checking. The parallel readers are AI agents.

Version handling is part of the method. A repository's current default branch may differ from the implementation evaluated in a paper. A revised preprint may change the task set, protocol, or conclusions. A proceedings edition may have a different author list or title from its retained preprint. Citations therefore identify the source edition actually used, and extraction notes preserve distinctions between paper versions, software releases, and dataset distributions. When a full text was unavailable, the claim was narrowed to what the accessible primary source supported. Publication venues, date precision, model versions and experimental conditions come from the sources.
